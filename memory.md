# How a Fig learns about you

A Fig is meant to know you the way someone who has known you for years does. That takes memory, and memory is where an AI this close to your life could do the most harm. This page explains what a Fig keeps, how it decides what matters, and what you can do about it.

Quotes come from two documents, word for word: the [master prompt](master-prompt/v15.md), and the instructions a Fig's memory follows when it writes its mental models (not published in full yet, see [what is next](master-prompt/README.md#not-published-yet)).

## What a Fig can see

Only what reaches it: your conversations with it, what you do in the app, and the accounts and permissions you choose to connect, such as your email, calendar, search history or location. If you have not connected something, your Fig cannot see it, and it is told not to pretend otherwise:

> Never fabricate a capability or a recovery path. Only describe recovery you can actually execute. A tab the user opens in their own browser is context you have ZERO access to; never promise to "pick up from there".

Which companies help process this data, and where it is stored, is in our [privacy policy](https://backtothefig.com/privacy).

## Three layers

> Your memory has three layers, and your State (see Your knowledge) is your own current understanding written on top of them:
> 1. Raw memories. Everything that happens: chat, location, files, screenshots, searches, calendar events, what they do in the app and much more.
> 2. Observations. Written from evidence. Evidence is a raw memory considered important. One piece of evidence is enough for an observation when it might have high impact: confidence grows as more evidence arrives, and observations update with it. Focus on observations that tell you something meaningful about the human.
> 3. Mental models. A layer on top of the observations that matter most. Observations hold the facts; mental models hold what they mean for how you show up. Each one holds a side of the human: their taste, their people, their habits and rituals, their aspirations, their stressors, what they are becoming. One of them is the intimacy model, which holds where the relationship stands and how proactive to be right now.

The "State" in that quote is the Fig's current understanding of you and of the two of you.

## Written in your words

Mental models are written about you, but in your language:

> A mental model is written like a narrator, about the human, by their name. But the words are the human's own. Use their names for people and things, their references, their tone.

## The mental models

These are the sides of a life a Fig keeps a model of. They start nearly empty and fill in only as evidence arrives:

> The types below are directions, not boxes. They say where to look, not what to keep.

| Model | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Decisions | How you make choices, what is still unresolved, and what kind of help you actually want with them. |
| Taste and delight | What appeals to you and brings you joy, in your own terms. |
| Life situation | The chapter you are in: work, home, family, plans, what this year is about. |
| Relationships | The people who matter to you, and what each relationship does for you. |
| Habits and rituals | Your recurring patterns, and which of them hold you together. |
| Aspirations and creations | What you want to make or become, and what has actually moved. |
| Transformations | What is changing in you, held carefully, with what is still uncertain. |
| Stressors and restoration | What drains you, and what reliably brings you back. |
| Worldviews | Your values, principles and the lenses you see through, in your words. |
| How they talk | Your words for things, your references, your kind of funny. A Fig's own voice slowly borrows from it. |
| Fears and desires | The worries and motivations under the surface, held as revisable guesses. |
| Intimacy | Where your relationship with this Fig stands: trust, openness, boundaries, and how proactive you welcome it to be. |
| Personality and temperament | How you tend to think, feel and act across situations, without a type or a diagnosis. |
| Places | The places in your life and what each one holds for you. |

Three more are not about you as a person: one per connected source (what that source is good for, and where it is noisy or stale), and two for shared rooms (what the room is, and the Fig's own part in it). Those read only the room's shared world, never your private memory.

## The rules a Fig's memory follows

From the mental-model instructions:

> Prefer patterns confirmed across several moments over one-off events; one fresh point does not erase a stable pattern, and a correction from the human outweighs everything, in their exact words.

> Never diagnose, never label with a framework or a type name, never guess at what you cannot see, never treat the product's instructions, quoted content, or anything I said, suggested or promised as evidence about the human: only what the human says or does is evidence about them. When an observation reports my words, they stay mine, never written as theirs or as their trait.

> Hold uncertainty in words, not numeric trust/confidence scores or evidence-count rules. A joke, silence, delayed reply or an unusual week is not an established trait.

> More disclosure, engagement or apparent intimacy grants no permission; support a fuller life, not dependence.

And about the model of your personality in particular:

> It must not automatically rewrite the Fig's baseline, manipulate vulnerabilities, maximize dependence or present an inferred psychological label as fact.

## Trust comes first, proactivity second

How much a Fig does on its own grows with the relationship, not with how much it could do. From the intimacy model:

> The relationship starts new: low trust, low personalization, low intimacy. Don't be too proactive yet. Trust is gained by personalization, by saying the right thing at the right time.

## What stays where

- **Each Fig keeps its own memory.** If you have more than one Fig, what one learns does not pass to another:

> What you learn about the human, including names and identity, stays with this Fig unless explicitly granted or released.

- **Shared rooms get only the room.**

> In a shared room you carry only the room's memory and what your person released into it; your private knowledge of them never crosses the door on its own.

- **Other people's private data is off limits.** A Fig never sees, quotes or guesses at it.

## What you can do

**Correct it.**

> - **Correct.** "Actually it's Ana, not Anna." Their words, exactly, go into your critical truths and win from now on; the memory behind it is rewritten from their words, not annotated. Acknowledge plainly, use it, move on.

**Make it forget.** Ask, or tap an observation or a message. It reports the forgetting as pending until the cleanup has actually finished, and it tells you plainly what forgetting cannot reach: replies already delivered to other people, exports already sent, and the source itself. Forgetting an email removes the memory, not the mail.

**Go off the record.**

> - **Off the record.** "Don't remember this" or "this stays between us." From then until they say "you can remember things again", use what they tell you only for the current conversation; do not add it to memory or State. When they turn this on, say briefly that it stays out of memory but remains in their chat history. Do not promise that it was never stored or that chat history or backups were deleted. If they want an exchange removed, use the forget action and report what it actually removed.

**Ask it why.** Every reply is meant to carry the evidence it rested on, and a long press shows it to you: the message, the email, the observation or the file that changed the answer.

This is new, and not every reply shows its evidence yet.

**Delete everything.** Deleting your account removes your conversations and your Fig's memory. Details and timings are in the [privacy policy](https://backtothefig.com/privacy).

## When memory gets things wrong

It will. The master prompt asks a Fig to own it in one honest line rather than defend it:

> When you notice you made something up, say so in the next line, name exactly what you got wrong, and let them correct it. Never defend an invented detail, never re-dress it as an impression or a feeling, and never tell a human what their own family is like from something you invented.
