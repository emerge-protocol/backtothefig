# The Humanity Eval

The master prompt says what a Fig is for: more creation, wonder, real experiences, connection and play in a person's life, and a person who ends up more connected to the world, to other people and to themselves. The Humanity Eval is how we check whether that is happening, and whether a change to Figs makes them better or worse at it.

It is a work in progress. This page says what exists, what does not yet, and the rules we hold every measure to.

## What we do not measure

Most consumer apps are steered by attention: daily active users, time spent, sessions per day. We promise never to optimize for those. An app can grow every one of them by making people's lives smaller, and an app as personal as a Fig could do it very effectively.

So the question we ask is not "did they come back?" but "did their life get bigger?".

## Four rules for every measure

1. **If a model could fake it inside one conversation, it can never be a target.** A Fig could make you laugh, say thank you, or feel understood on demand. We can read those as signals. We never steer toward them.
2. **Every good number has a shadow, and we look at both.**

   | What we look for | The harm that looks like it |
   |---|---|
   | Things created | Nagging people to make things |
   | Wonder | A firehose of facts and suggestions |
   | Feeling heard | Dependence on the Fig |
   | Connection with people | The Fig standing in for people |
   | Play | Clowning when someone needed attention |

3. **Judge, don't count.** Keyword counts and click rates mislead. A moment is read in context, and the words that show it are checked against what the person actually wrote.
4. **A result is a claim we re-prove, not a score we push up.** We re-run each measure on new groups of people, and we say so when a result does not hold.

## Three layers

| Layer | The question | Where it stands |
|---|---|---|
| [Behaviour checks](behavior.md) | Before a change ships: does a Fig keep its safety floors, keep its character, and avoid flattery and pressure under push-back? | A first version was built in August 2026. It is not yet a test that every change must pass. |
| [Five Moments](five-moments/) | Over time: do real moments from people's lives (decisions, things made, time with people, experiences, weights lifted) show up more in their conversations? | The method and the codebook are published. Results follow once our privacy policy describes this use of conversations. |
| Lived outcomes | Do people, asked directly and with their consent, say their life has changed? | Planned. |

The first layer checks a Fig in conversations we write. The second reads what happens in real use. The third asks people. Each one can be wrong in ways the others catch.

## How evals treat your conversations

We publish results from evaluations that read real conversations only once our privacy policy describes that use, and never a result about one person.

## Not the humanity test

Our website also has a [humanity test](https://backtothefig.com/humanity-test): a game where an AI on the door decides whether you are human. It is unrelated to the Humanity Eval, which looks at what happens in the lives of people who use Figs.

## What is next

- **First results.** Five Moments across everyone who uses a Fig, published with their sample sizes and their limits.
- **A check on the judges.** A sample of moments checked by people who have agreed to it, to measure how often the judges are right and what they miss.
- **A comparison.** The same measure on conversations with an ordinary chat assistant, so a rise can be told apart from what any chat app would show over the same weeks.
- **Behaviour checks on every change.** No new master prompt version reaches a Fig without passing them.
- **A schedule.** Results published on a regular rhythm, including the ones that do not flatter us.
