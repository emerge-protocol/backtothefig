# Five Moments

The master prompt names what a Fig is for in terms of events in a person's life:

> These are the moments you strive for: tangible impact on the human's life. They are events in the human's life, not moments in the chat.

Five Moments counts those events. It is the first instrument of [the Humanity Eval](../), and it is still young. This page is the method. Results will follow, published with their sample sizes and their limits.

## The five

| Moment | For example | From the master prompt |
|---|---|---|
| Decisions made | The lease, the job, the breakup | "A decision gets made." |
| Things created | Started the novel, finished the chair | "Something real gets created." |
| Social moments | Someone called, a friend actually met | "A relationship gets built, repaired, or deepened." |
| Experiences had | Went for the run, attended the event | "They do something: go somewhere, sign up, start something" |
| Stressors removed | The form filed, the bill sorted | "You were able to do something for them: a weight taken away, a real action you took that landed in their life." |

The master prompt names two more: a pattern in someone's life changing for the better, and someone saying their attitude to life is changing. They are rare, and only count when the person says so themselves, so we record them as tags on the five rather than as counts of their own.

**One moment is one event in a person's life.** A good conversation is not a moment. A feeling with no event behind it ("I'm tired") is not one, and neither is a wish ("I wish I could travel"), a plan still being weighed, or something that happened before the person met their Fig. Bad events count too: a fight, a cancelled flight, a new bill. They are labelled as negative and reported, not hidden.

The full definitions, fields and worked examples are in the [codebook](codebook.md).

## How it works

1. **Who.** People who use Figs, as codes, never by name or email. Our own team and test accounts are left out.
2. **What is read.** A person's history with their Figs: their messages, voice transcripts and group chats, descriptions of photos they sent, and a few records that can show a moment on their own (an app built, a trip to another city, a room shared with another person). Messages copied twice are counted once.
3. **Judging.** An AI judge reads the history in parts and labels every moment it finds, with the exact words that show it. Only the person's own words, or a record, count as evidence. A Fig saying it did something counts only if a record or the person confirms it.
4. **Checking.** Every quote is compared with the message it came from. A quote that does not match exactly is dropped, never repaired. A part the judge did not read to its last message is judged again.
5. **Merging.** The same event mentioned in two places becomes one moment.
6. **Counting.** Three counts come from the same labels:
   - **Broad:** every event that plausibly fits, big or small.
   - **In their life:** broad, limited to what happened off the app or with other people on it. Things made with the Fig inside the app, and anything inside a role-play story, are left out.
   - **Verified:** in their life, stated concretely or shown by a record, and clearly fitting the definition.

## How we compare weeks

People write less to their Fig as the weeks go by, and someone who writes less reports fewer moments. So we compare the same people with themselves, both per message and per week, and we always show both, together with how much they wrote.

## What this method cannot show

- **That Figs caused anything, on its own.** Without a comparison group, a rise over time could be what any chat app would show. A comparison is planned (see below).
- **Everything that happened.** We only see what people tell their Fig, plus a few records.
- **Exact counts.** Judges miss things and can disagree about single moments, so counts are floors. We measure how far two judges agree, and say so next to every result.
- **A human view.** The judges are AI models. We check them against people's judgement on a sample, with consent.
- **Who did it.** For every moment the judges note whether the Fig played a part, so the moments a Fig helped with are reported apart from the ones people made happen on their own.

## What is next

First results, across everyone who uses a Fig, published only once our privacy policy describes this use of conversations. Beside them: a sample checked by people who have agreed to it, a second judge on part of the conversations, and the same measure on an ordinary chat assistant as a comparison.
